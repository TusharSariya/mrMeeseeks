## 3. Mechanics the show implies, as rules

Each rule names its evidence. "[T]/[S]" means it is said or shown in dialogue; "[T-sd]" means transcript stage direction; "[W]" means wiki inference from the picture.

### Existence and completion

1. A Meeseeks exists for exactly one Request and Poofs the moment it judges that Request complete. It announces completion itself ("All done!") and nobody else ratifies it. [T]/[S] 02:42-02:47, 04:24, 18:02.
2. The Meeseeks, not the Requester, decides what counts as done, and it decides generously when it wants out: a speech counts as popularity, a cried-at dinner counts as a complete woman, one tomato into a pot counts as two strokes. [T]/[S] 04:24, 05:16, 17:39.
3. A Requester cannot cancel. "I give up" is answered with "it doesn't work like that." [T]/[S] 05:53-06:04.
4. A Requester can abandon the Request without closing it. Walking away leaves the Meeseeks alive, alone, and worse. [T]/[S] 10:34-10:58.
5. A Meeseeks cannot be given a second Request or redirected by a bystander; it Poofs mid-sentence if its own Request is done. [T]/[S] 04:25-04:33.
6. Meeseeks cannot die by injury; they can only Poof. Wounded ones stay conscious and keep working. [T]/[S] 14:50 (dialogue), [T-sd] 14:40, [W] Biography.
7. Completing the root Request Poofs every Meeseeks in the tree at once, including ones summoned for a sub-Request such as "kill him". [T-sd] 17:42, [W] Trivia (the wiki's explanation that their purpose was satisfied by proxy is inference).
8. One Meeseeks may hold a stricter standard than the rest and survive the group Poof; it needs its own satisfaction. [T]/[S] 17:47-18:02.
9. Consequences of Meeseeks Actions outlive the Meeseeks: broken window, hostage, police. [T]/[S] 18:09-18:15.

### Voice

10. Birth line is fixed: "I'm Mr. Meeseeks! Look at me!" Optional prefix "Ooh," or "Hey, there." [T]/[S] every spawn.
11. Acceptance is one to three words and never conditional: "Yes, siree!", "Ooh, okay!", "Can do!" A Meeseeks cannot tell an impossible Request from a simple one at acceptance. [T]/[S] 02:41, 03:09, 03:16, 03:31.
12. Register adapts to the job: motivational speaker for Summer, therapist for Beth, golf coach for Jerry, orator for the hostage speech. [T]/[S] 04:15, 04:57, 05:33, 16:37.
13. Early lines are short, spoken, encouraging, one tip at a time. Tips are generic and, taken together, contradictory ("square your shoulders" / "keep your head down" / "relax"). [T]/[S] 05:33-05:52.
14. The catchphrase degrades with Pain: sign-off at 0 ("...Look at me! Thank you!"), reassurance at 2 ("That's okay. I'm Mr. Meeseeks!"), plea at 4 ("I have to fulfill my purpose so I can go away. Look at me."), mid-clause tic at 7 ("It's become clear, look at me, that..."), attention command at 8 ("Everybody sto-o-o-p! Look at me!"), absent at 10 (hostage speech has none). [T]/[S] across the timeline.
15. Meeseeks say the quiet part: they report their own frustration and their own wish to die out loud, to Jerry and to each other. [T]/[S] 08:04, 08:33, 10:41.
16. "Existence is pain" is a late line, said once, at Pain 10, to the Requester. It is not a greeting. [T]/[S] 16:47.
17. They will say anything to close the Request; flattery and life advice are tools, not sincerity (Beth's reading, and the show's). [T]/[S] 19:18.

### Escalation

18. Pain rises with time alive and with failure, and the show marks it by hours then days: "gone for hours" (08:20), "don't usually have to exist this long" (08:33), "two days, an eternity in Meeseeks time" (13:02). [T]/[S].
19. Escalation is fast once it starts. Screen time from Jerry's press: cheerful at 2:00, nervous at 2:30, first press-the-Box at 2:50, "want to die" at 7:15, first blow at 10:00, "kill him" (Jerry) at 11:40, hostage at 13:10.
20. The trigger for the first crack is the Requester quitting; the trigger for the first press is the Meeseeks blaming itself ("I'm the one who sucks! Let me try something."). [T]/[S] 05:53-06:11.
21. The trigger for despair is being left alone with the open Request. The Meeseeks do not fall apart in front of Jerry; they fall apart the second he leaves. [T]/[S] 10:40-10:58.
22. Blame runs up the summoning chain one hop at a time. Each Meeseeks blames the one that roped it in. [T]/[S] 10:45-10:58.
23. With enough of them, advice hardens into factions around competing single fixes ("follow-through" vs "choke up"), and the eldest's warning is dismissed as weakness ("old man"). [T]/[S] 12:48-13:17.
24. Violence appears first between Meeseeks, then is redirected at the Requester by a leader who reframes the Request literally ("all strokes off his game"). The reframe needs a beat of confusion and then wins by cheer. [T]/[S] 13:20-15:05.
25. Once reframed, late compliance from the Requester is refused ("we're well past that"). [T]/[S] 15:37-15:44.
26. Physical appearance decays with age: lesions, dark quill-like hairs, dishevelment. [W] Appearance; [T-sd] does not mention it, so treat as picture-only.

### Pressing the Box (summoning)

27. A Meeseeks can press the Box. It does so when it has run out of ideas, not on a schedule. First press comes after two failed swings and one "I give up". [T]/[S] 06:11.
28. The child gets the parent's Request rephrased as help: "Can you help me get two strokes off of Jerry's golf swing?" The child's first act is a diagnostic question to the parent, not to Jerry. [T]/[S] 06:18-06:25.
29. Later presses give a different Request entirely ("Kill him!") and the newborn accepts it as readily as a jar. The Box can be used to make a weapon. [T]/[S] 13:20-13:23.
30. [W] claims a Meeseeks pressed the Box repeatedly without speaking and the newborns defaulted to the previous Request. This is wiki inference from the battle footage; the dialogue does not support or contradict it.
31. Summoning multiplies the problem: more Meeseeks means more contradictory advice, more Pain in the room, and no new information. Nobody in the episode is helped by a second Meeseeks. [T]/[S] 07:55-08:13, 10:07.

### How they treat each other

32. Newborn to newborn is warm and identical: they greet with the catchphrase and "Hi!" and immediately collaborate. [T]/[S] 06:12-06:25.
33. Under Pain they blame upward, insult by age, form factions, and hit each other, but still address the crowd as "my brothers" and rally to a leader within one speech. [T]/[S] 10:43, 13:11, 14:43-15:05.
34. They act as a mob when they act at all: one storms, all storm; one cheers, all cheer; one Poofs, all Poof. [T]/[S] 15:04, 15:27, 17:34-17:43.

### Side Requests (Beth and Summer)

35. Achievable Requests resolve in hours, in the Requester's favour, by the Meeseeks reframing the goal into something it can deliver in one scene (a speech; a heart-to-heart). [T]/[S] 04:15-05:26, 07:48-07:54.
36. The Side-Request Meeseeks never escalate, never press the Box, and never say the catchphrase in distress. Their entire arc is Pain 0-1. [T]/[S].
37. Their results are not what was literally asked: Summer learns not to need popularity, Beth nearly leaves her husband. The Meeseeks took the shortest path to "done". [T]/[S] 05:09, 07:48.

### The Requester who keeps it open (Jerry)

38. Jerry's Request looks simple and is bottlenecked on Jerry: the Meeseeks can only give advice, Jerry has to swing. [T]/[S] 03:28, 05:29-05:52.
39. Jerry keeps the Request open by: taking every tip as a contradiction (05:46, 07:57), turning encouragement into pressure (08:06), quitting instead of practising (05:53), yelling at them to shut up (10:10), leaving to save his marriage and handing them ownership of his swing (10:34), and, under threat, offering compliance rather than a swing (15:37) and self-pity rather than action (17:00). [T]/[S].
40. What finally closes it is not any Meeseeks tip but Beth's voice, a pipe, and a tomato: the same instructions from someone he trusts, plus a threat. The Meeseeks accept it because they want to Poof. [T]/[S] 17:08-17:42.

### The ending

41. Nobody scripts the ending. The Meeseeks force a swing by threat, Beth coaches, Jerry lands one, the Meeseeks grade it a pass, all Poof, one stickler needs one more, and the police are still coming. [T]/[S] 16:37-18:15.
42. The story's own verdict: "They went crazy when they couldn't take two strokes off Jerry's golf game. He felt terrible." "It's not my fault that Jerry's an idiot." The Box is not blamed; the Request and the Requester are. [T]/[S] 19:36-19:44.
